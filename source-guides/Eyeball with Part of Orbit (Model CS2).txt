Eye

7
    Identification→Sclera #Identification #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Tough opaque fibrous outer coat of the posterior five-sixths of the eyeball. #Structure #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Function→Maintains globe shape, protects intraocular contents and provides attachment for extraocular muscles. #Function #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Relations→Continuous anteriorly with cornea and posteriorly with the dural sheath of the optic nerve. #Relations #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    
8
    Identification→Cornea #Identification #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Transparent avascular anterior part of the fibrous coat of the eye. #Structure #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Function→Provides most of the eye's refractive power and transmits light into the anterior chamber. #Function #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Innervation→Sensory innervation mainly via long ciliary nerves from nasociliary nerve (V1). #Innervation #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    
9
    Identification→Choroid #Identification #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Highly vascular pigmented layer between sclera and retina, forming part of the vascular tunic. #Structure #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Function→Supplies outer retina and absorbs stray light. #Function #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Relations→Continuous anteriorly with the ciliary body. #Relations #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    
10
    Identification→Iris #Identification #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Pigmented contractile diaphragm anterior to the lens with a central opening, the pupil. #Structure #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Function→Regulates the amount of light entering the eye by changing pupil diameter. #Function #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Innervation→Parasympathetic fibres constrict the pupil via sphincter pupillae; sympathetic fibres dilate it via dilator pupillae. #Innervation #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    
11
    Identification→Retina #Identification #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Inner neural layer of the eye containing photoreceptors and retinal neurons. #Structure #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Function→Converts light into neural signals transmitted via the optic nerve. #Function #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    Blood supply→Inner retina supplied mainly by central retinal artery; outer retina by choroidal circulation. #BloodSupply #EyeLayer #Eye #CS2
    
12
    Identification→Vitreous humour (vitreous body) #Identification #EyeContent #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Transparent gel filling the vitreous chamber posterior to the lens. #Structure #EyeContent #Eye #CS2
    Function→Maintains globe shape, supports the retina and transmits light to the retina. #Function #EyeContent #Eye #CS2
    Relations→Lies between the lens and retina. #Relations #EyeContent #Eye #CS2
    
13
    Identification→Lens #Identification #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Transparent biconvex avascular structure suspended by zonular fibres from the ciliary body. #Structure #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Function→Focuses light on the retina and changes shape during accommodation. #Function #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Relations→Lies posterior to iris and anterior to vitreous body. #Relations #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    
14
    Identification→Optic nerve sheath meets sclera #Identification #Landmark #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Posterior junction where the meningeal coverings of the optic nerve become continuous with the sclera of the globe. #Structure #Landmark #Eye #CS2
    Relations→Surrounds the optic nerve as it enters the posterior eyeball. #Relations #Landmark #Eye #CS2
    Clinical relevance→Subarachnoid space around the optic nerve is continuous with intracranial CSF space, so raised intracranial pressure can produce papilloedema. #Clinical #Landmark #Eye #CS2
    
15
    Identification→Ciliary nerves entering through sclera #Identification #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Course→Long and short ciliary nerves pierce the sclera around the optic nerve and pass forward within the suprachoroidal space. #Course #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Sensory functions→Carry sensory fibres from cornea and other ocular structures. #Sensory #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Autonomic functions→Carry sympathetic and parasympathetic fibres to structures including iris and ciliary muscle. #Autonomic #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Lesion→Damage may impair ocular sensation and autonomic control of the pupil and accommodation, depending on which long or short ciliary fibres are involved.     #Lesion #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    
16
    Identification→Optic nerve (CN II) #Identification #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Roots→Formed by axons of retinal ganglion cells. #Roots #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Course→Leaves the posterior globe, passes through the optic canal and reaches the optic chiasm. #Course #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Sensory functions→Vision and afferent limb of the pupillary light reflex. #Sensory #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Lesion→Lesion causes ipsilateral monocular visual loss and an afferent pupillary defect. #Lesion #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    
17
    Identification→Ciliary muscle #Identification #Muscle #Eye #CS2
    Actions→Contracts during accommodation, reducing tension on zonular fibres so the lens becomes more convex for near vision. #Action #Muscle #Eye #CS2
    Innervation→Parasympathetic fibres from oculomotor nerve via ciliary ganglion and short ciliary nerves. #Innervation #Muscle #Eye #CS2
    Location→Smooth muscle within the ciliary body. #Location #Muscle #Eye #CS2
    
18
    Identification→Ciliary body #Identification #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Structure→Anterior continuation of the choroid containing ciliary muscle and ciliary processes. #Structure #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Function→Produces aqueous humour and controls lens shape through the ciliary muscle and zonular fibres. #Function #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2
    Relations→Lies between choroid posteriorly and iris anteriorly. #Relations #EyeStructure #Eye #CS2

20
    Identification→Long and short ciliary nerves #Identification #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Roots→Long ciliary nerves arise from nasociliary nerve (V1); short ciliary nerves arise from the ciliary ganglion. #Roots #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Course→Pierce sclera posteriorly and run forward between sclera and choroid. #Course #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Sensory functions→Carry sensory fibres from the eye, especially cornea via long ciliary pathways. #Sensory #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Autonomic functions→Short ciliary nerves carry parasympathetic fibres to sphincter pupillae and ciliary muscle; sympathetic fibres travel in both long and short ciliary nerves. #Autonomic #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    Lesion→Depending on which fibres are affected, may impair corneal sensation, pupillary constriction or dilation, and accommodation.     #Lesion #Nerve #Eye #CS2
    
21
    Identification→Macula #Identification #RetinalLandmark #Eye #CS2
    Location→Specialised area of retina lateral to the optic disc; fovea centralis lies at its centre. #Location #RetinalLandmark #Eye #CS2
    Function→Responsible for high-acuity central vision and detailed colour vision. #Function #RetinalLandmark #Eye #CS2
    
22
    Identification→Retinal artery #Identification #Artery #Eye #CS2
    Origin→Central retinal artery is usually a branch of the ophthalmic artery. #Origin #Artery #Eye #CS2

23
    Identification→Retinal vein #Identification #Vein #Eye #CS2
    Course→Central retinal vein drains the retinal venous circulation and leaves through the optic nerve. #Course #Vein #Eye #CS2
    Drainage→Drains blood from the retina into the ophthalmic venous system/cavernous sinus circulation. #Drainage #Vein #Eye #CS2
    Clinical relevance→Central retinal vein occlusion can cause sudden painless visual impairment with widespread retinal haemorrhages. #Clinical #Vein #Eye #CS2
