Major landmarks
A
    Identification→Apex of the heart #Identification #CardiacLandmark #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    Position→Points forwards, downwards and to the left; in adults it usually lies deep to the left 5th intercostal space near the midclavicular line, about 9 cm from the median plane. #Position #CardiacLandmark #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    Formation→Formed mainly by the left ventricle. #Structure #CardiacLandmark #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    Clinical relevance→The apex beat is normally palpable in the left 5th intercostal space near the midclavicular line. #Clinical #CardiacLandmark #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    
B
    Identification→Interventricular septum #Identification #CardiacStructure #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    Function→Separates the right and left ventricles and allows the two circulations to operate at different pressures. #Function #CardiacStructure #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    Parts→Has a large muscular part and a small membranous part. #Parts #CardiacStructure #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    Relations→Traversed by components of the cardiac conduction system, including the atrioventricular bundle and bundle branches. #Relations #CardiacStructure #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Ventricular septal defects most commonly involve the membranous region. #Clinical #CardiacStructure #MajorLandmarks #HS5
    
Chambers
I
    Identification→Right atrium #Identification #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Function→Receives systemic venous blood and delivers it to the right ventricle. #Function #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Inflow→Receives blood from the superior vena cava, inferior vena cava and coronary sinus. #Contents #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Outflow→Blood passes through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle. #Connections #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Internal features→Includes sinus venarum, pectinate muscles, crista terminalis and fossa ovalis. #Landmarks #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    
II
    Identification→Left atrium #Identification #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Function→Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs and delivers it to the left ventricle. #Function #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Inflow→Usually receives four pulmonary veins. #Contents #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Outflow→Blood passes through the mitral valve into the left ventricle. #Connections #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Internal features→Most of the atrial wall is smooth; pectinate muscles are mainly confined to the left auricle. #Landmarks #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    
III
    Identification→Right ventricle #Identification #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Function→Pumps deoxygenated blood into the pulmonary circulation. #Function #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Inflow→Receives blood from the right atrium through the tricuspid valve. #Contents #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Outflow→Ejects blood through the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary trunk. #Connections #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Internal features→Contains trabeculae carneae, papillary muscles, chordae tendineae and the moderator band. #Landmarks #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Wall thickness→Thinner than the left ventricle because pulmonary vascular resistance is lower. #Relations #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    
IV
    Identification→Left ventricle #Identification #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Function→Pumps oxygenated blood into the systemic circulation. #Function #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Inflow→Receives blood from the left atrium through the mitral valve. #Contents #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Outflow→Ejects blood through the aortic valve into the ascending aorta. #Connections #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Internal features→Contains trabeculae carneae, papillary muscles and chordae tendineae. #Landmarks #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    Wall thickness→Has the thickest myocardium because it pumps against systemic vascular resistance. #Relations #HeartChamber #Chambers #HS5
    
Auricles, great vessels and valves
a
    Identification→Right auricle #Identification #Auricle #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Structure→Muscular pouch projecting from the right atrium and containing pectinate muscles. #Structure #Auricle #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Function→Increases the capacity of the right atrium. #Function #Auricle #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
b
    Identification→Left auricle #Identification #Auricle #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Structure→Muscular appendage of the left atrium containing pectinate muscles. #Structure #Auricle #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Function→Increases the capacity of the left atrium. #Function #Auricle #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Clinical relevance→The left atrial appendage is a common site of thrombus formation in atrial fibrillation. #Clinical #Auricle #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
c
    Identification→Superior vena cava #Identification #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Formation→Formed by union of the right and left brachiocephalic veins. #Formation #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Course→Descends in the right superior mediastinum and enters the superior aspect of the right atrium. #Course #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Drainage→Returns venous blood from structures above the diaphragm except the heart and lungs. #Drainage #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
d
    Identification→Inferior vena cava #Identification #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Formation→Formed by union of the common iliac veins, usually at L5. #Formation #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Course→Ascends through the diaphragm at T8 and enters the inferior aspect of the right atrium. #Course #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Drainage→Returns venous blood from structures below the diaphragm. #Drainage #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
e
    Identification→Tricuspid valve (right atrioventricular valve) #Identification #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Location→Between right atrium and right ventricle. #Location #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Structure→Usually has anterior, posterior and septal cusps attached to chordae tendineae and papillary muscles. #Structure #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Function→Prevents backflow from right ventricle to right atrium during ventricular systole. #Function #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Regurgitation may produce prominent systolic venous pulsation and right-sided volume overload. #Clinical #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
f
    Identification→Pulmonary trunk #Identification #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Origin→Arises from right ventricle through the pulmonary valve. #Origin #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Course→Passes superiorly and posteriorly before dividing into right and left pulmonary arteries. #Course #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Supply→Carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs. #Supply #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
g
    Identification→Pulmonary veins #Identification #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Drainage→Return oxygenated blood from lungs to the left atrium. #Drainage #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Number→Usually four pulmonary veins, two from each lung. #Structure #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Pulmonary venous ostia are important sites of ectopic activity in atrial fibrillation. #Clinical #Vein #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
h
    Identification→Bicuspid (mitral) valve #Identification #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Location→Between left atrium and left ventricle. #Location #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Structure→Has anterior and posterior cusps attached to chordae tendineae and papillary muscles. #Structure #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Function→Prevents backflow from left ventricle into left atrium during ventricular systole. #Function #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Mitral stenosis or regurgitation may cause left atrial enlargement and pulmonary congestion. #Clinical #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
i
    Identification→Aorta #Identification #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Origin→Arises from left ventricle through the aortic valve. #Origin #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Parts→Ascending aorta, arch of aorta and descending thoracic/abdominal aorta. #Parts #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Function→Main systemic artery carrying oxygenated blood from the left ventricle. #Function #Artery #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
k
    Identification→Semilunar valves #Identification #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Components→The aortic and pulmonary valves are semilunar valves. #Components #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Structure→Each has three pocket-shaped cusps and no chordae tendineae or papillary muscle attachments. #Structure #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Function→Prevent backflow from the aorta and pulmonary trunk into the ventricles during diastole. #Function #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Aortic stenosis causes left ventricular pressure overload; pulmonary valve disease affects right ventricular outflow. #Clinical #Valve #AuriclesVesselsValves #HS5
    
Internal and coronary structures
1
    Identification→Chordae tendineae #Identification #CardiacStructure #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Structure→Fibrous cords connecting atrioventricular valve cusps to papillary muscles. #Structure #CardiacStructure #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Function→Prevent prolapse or eversion of AV valve cusps during ventricular systole. #Function #CardiacStructure #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Relations→Present in tricuspid and mitral valves, but not semilunar valves. #Relations #CardiacStructure #InternalCoronary #HS5
    
2
    Identification→Papillary muscles #Identification #Muscle #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Actions→Contract during ventricular systole to tense chordae tendineae and stabilise AV valve cusps. #Action #Muscle #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Function→Prevent valve prolapse rather than actively closing the valve. #Function #Muscle #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Relations→Right ventricle usually has anterior, posterior and septal papillary muscles; left ventricle has anterolateral and posteromedial papillary muscles. #Relations #Muscle #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Papillary muscle dysfunction or rupture can cause acute atrioventricular valve regurgitation. #Clinical #Muscle #InternalCoronary #HS5
    
3
    Identification→Ascending aorta #Identification #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Origin→Begins at the aortic valve of the left ventricle. #Origin #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Course→Ascends within the pericardium and continues as the arch of the aorta. #Course #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Branches→Right and left coronary arteries arise from the aortic sinuses. #Branches #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Supply→Carries oxygenated blood into the systemic circulation. #Supply #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    
4
    Identification→Right coronary artery #Identification #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Origin→Arises from the right aortic sinus. #Origin #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Course→Runs in the right atrioventricular groove and continues toward the inferior surface of the heart. #Course #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Branches→Common branches include right marginal artery and, in right-dominant hearts, the posterior interventricular artery. #Branches #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Supply→Typically supplies right atrium, most of right ventricle, part of left ventricle, posterior third of interventricular septum and often SA/AV nodes. #Supply #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Occlusion may cause inferior myocardial infarction and can affect AV nodal conduction. #Clinical #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    
5
    Identification→Left coronary artery #Identification #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Origin→Arises from the left aortic sinus. #Origin #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Course→Passes between pulmonary trunk and left auricle before dividing. #Course #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Branches→Usually divides into anterior interventricular (LAD) and circumflex arteries. #Branches #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Supply→Supplies most of left atrium and ventricle, anterior right ventricle and anterior two-thirds of interventricular septum. #Supply #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Proximal LAD occlusion can cause a large anterior myocardial infarction. #Clinical #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    
6
    Identification→Coronary sinus #Identification #VenousSinus #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Location→Runs in the posterior atrioventricular groove. #Location #VenousSinus #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Drainage→Receives most venous blood from myocardium, including via great, middle and small cardiac veins. #Drainage #VenousSinus #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Termination→Opens into the right atrium between the inferior vena cava opening and tricuspid orifice. #Termination #VenousSinus #InternalCoronary #HS5
    
7
    Identification→Arch of the aorta #Identification #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Course→Continues from ascending aorta, arches superiorly, posteriorly and to the left, then continues as descending thoracic aorta. #Course #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Branches→Brachiocephalic trunk, left common carotid artery and left subclavian artery. #Branches #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Relations→Passes over the left main bronchus; left recurrent laryngeal nerve loops beneath the arch near ligamentum arteriosum. #Relations #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
    Clinical relevance→Aneurysm may compress the left recurrent laryngeal nerve and cause hoarseness. #Clinical #Artery #InternalCoronary #HS5
