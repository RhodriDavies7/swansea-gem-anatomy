1
    Identification→Pubic bone #Identification #Bone #MS1
    Articulations→Articulates with the opposite pubis at the pubic symphysis; joins the ilium and ischium within the hip bone and contributes to the acetabulum. #Articulations #Bone #MS1
    Landmarks→Body, superior ramus, inferior ramus, pubic crest, pubic tubercle, pecten pubis and obturator groove. #Landmarks #Bone #MS1
    Muscle attachments→Pectineus, adductor longus, adductor brevis, gracilis and part of adductor magnus attach to the pubis; rectus abdominis attaches near the pubic crest/symphysis. #Attachments #Bone #MS1
    Orientation→Symphyseal surface faces medially; body lies anteriorly; superior ramus lies above the obturator foramen. #Orientation #Bone #MS1
    
2
    Identification→Sacral vertebrae #Identification #Bone #MS1
    Articulations→The fused sacral vertebrae form the sacrum, articulating with L5 superiorly, the ilia laterally and the coccyx inferiorly. #Articulations #Bone #MS1
    Landmarks→Sacral promontory, alae, anterior and posterior sacral foramina, median sacral crest, auricular surfaces, sacral canal and sacral hiatus. #Landmarks #Bone #MS1
    Muscle attachments→Piriformis arises from the pelvic surface; gluteus maximus and multifidus attach posteriorly; pelvic floor muscles attach to the lower lateral sacrum. #Attachments #Bone #MS1
    Orientation→Broad base superior and apex inferior; smooth concave pelvic surface anterior, rough crested surface posterior. #Orientation #Bone #MS1
    
3
    Identification→Coccygeal vertebrae #Identification #Bone #MS1
    Articulations→Form the coccyx and articulate superiorly with the sacrum at the sacrococcygeal joint. #Articulations #Bone #MS1
    Landmarks→Usually 3–5 small vertebral segments with coccygeal cornua and transverse processes most evident on the first segment. #Landmarks #Bone #MS1
    Muscle attachments→Coccygeus, levator ani and gluteus maximus attach to the coccyx. #Attachments #Bone #MS1
    Orientation→Base faces superiorly toward the sacrum and the apex points inferiorly, usually curving anteriorly. #Orientation #Bone #MS1
    
4
    Identification→Labium majus #Identification #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Function→Forms the lateral boundary of the pudendal cleft and protects the more medial external genital structures. #Function #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Structure→Paired skin folds containing fat, smooth muscle and connective tissue; homologous to the male scrotum. #Structure #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Relations→Extends from the mons pubis anteriorly toward the perineum posteriorly, lateral to the labium minus. #Relations #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Innervation→Anterior parts receive ilioinguinal and genital branch of genitofemoral nerves; posterior parts receive posterior labial branches of pudendal nerve. #Innervation #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    
5
    Identification→Labium minus #Identification #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Function→Borders the vestibule and helps protect the urethral and vaginal openings. #Function #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Structure→Paired thin, hairless skin folds without substantial subcutaneous fat. #Structure #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Relations→Lies medial to the labium majus and surrounds the vestibule; anteriorly contributes to the prepuce and frenulum of the clitoris. #Relations #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    Innervation→Mainly posterior labial branches of pudendal nerve, with anterior contributions from ilioinguinal/genitofemoral territories. #Innervation #ExternalGenitalia #MS1
    
6
    Identification→Clitoris #Identification #ErectileTissue #MS1
    Function→Erectile sensory organ involved in sexual arousal. #Function #ErectileTissue #MS1
    Parts→Root, body and glans; paired crura form the root. #Parts #ErectileTissue #MS1
    Relations→Located at the anterior end of the vulva where the labia minora meet. #Relations #ErectileTissue #MS1
    Blood supply→Branches of the internal pudendal artery, including deep and dorsal arteries of the clitoris. #BloodSupply #ErectileTissue #MS1
    Innervation→Somatic sensation mainly via the dorsal nerve of the clitoris; autonomic cavernous fibres regulate erection. #Innervation #ErectileTissue #MS1
    
7
    Identification→Urethra #Identification #Tube #MS1
    Function→Conducts urine from the urinary bladder to the external urethral orifice. #Function #Tube #MS1
    Course→Short tube passing inferoanteriorly from the bladder neck through the pelvic floor to open in the vestibule. #Course #Tube #MS1
    Relations→Lies anterior to the vagina and is closely related to its anterior wall. #Relations #Tube #MS1
    Clinical relevance→The short female urethra contributes to a higher risk of ascending urinary tract infection. #Clinical #Tube #MS1
    
8
    Identification→Urinary bladder #Identification #Organ #MS1
    Function→Stores urine and expels it during micturition. #Function #Organ #MS1
    Parts→Apex, body, fundus/base and neck; the internal surface includes the trigone. #Parts #Organ #MS1
    Relations→Lies posterior to the pubic symphysis and anterior to the uterus and vagina; the superior surface is related to peritoneum. #Relations #Organ #MS1
    Innervation→Parasympathetic pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2–S4) contract detrusor and promote voiding; sympathetic fibres from T11–L2 favour storage. #Innervation #Organ #MS1
    Blood supply→Mainly superior vesical arteries, with additional vesical branches from internal iliac derivatives. #BloodSupply #Organ #MS1
    
9
    Identification→Ureter #Identification #Tube #MS1
    Function→Transports urine from the renal pelvis to the urinary bladder by peristalsis. #Function #Tube #MS1
    Course→Descends retroperitoneally on psoas, crosses the pelvic brim near the iliac vessels, then runs on the lateral pelvic wall and enters the bladder obliquely. #Course #Tube #MS1
    Constrictions→At the pelviureteric junction, where it crosses the iliac vessels/pelvic brim, and at the vesicoureteric junction. #Constrictions #Tube #MS1
    Relations→In the female pelvis, passes inferior to the uterine artery near the lateral fornix of the vagina. #Relations #Tube #MS1
    Clinical relevance→Its relationship to the uterine artery is important during hysterectomy because the ureter may be injured. #Clinical #Tube #MS1
    
10
    Identification→Vagina #Identification #Organ #MS1
    Function→Fibromuscular canal for intercourse, passage of menstrual flow and childbirth. #Function #Organ #MS1
    Course→Extends from the vaginal vestibule superiorly to surround the cervix. #Course #Organ #MS1
    Relations→Anterior to rectum and posterior to urethra/bladder; superiorly surrounds cervix to form vaginal fornices. #Relations #Organ #MS1
    Innervation→Upper vagina receives visceral autonomic innervation; lower vagina receives somatic innervation mainly via pudendal nerve. #Innervation #Organ #MS1
    Blood supply→Vaginal and uterine arteries, with contributions from internal pudendal and middle rectal branches. #BloodSupply #Organ #MS1
    
11
    Identification→Uterus #Identification #Organ #MS1
    Function→Site of implantation and development of the embryo/fetus; myometrium contracts during labour. #Function #Organ #MS1
    Parts→Fundus, body, isthmus and cervix. #Parts #Organ #MS1
    Position→Normally anteverted relative to the vagina and anteflexed at the junction of body and cervix. #Position #Organ #MS1
    Relations→Bladder lies anteriorly and rectum posteriorly; peritoneal reflections form vesicouterine and rectouterine pouches. #Relations #Organ #MS1
    Blood supply→Uterine artery from internal iliac artery, with anastomoses to ovarian and vaginal arteries. #BloodSupply #Organ #MS1
    Lymphatic drainage→Fundus can drain to para-aortic nodes and via round ligament to superficial inguinal nodes; body mainly to external iliac nodes; cervix mainly to internal iliac and sacral nodes. #Lymphatics #Organ #MS1
    
12
    Identification→Cervix of the uterus #Identification #OrganPart #MS1
    Organ→Inferior cylindrical part of the uterus projecting into the vagina. #Organ #OrganPart #MS1
    Parts→Supravaginal and vaginal parts. #Parts #OrganPart #MS1
    Relations→Vaginal fornices surround the vaginal part; bladder lies anteriorly and rectum posteriorly. #Relations #OrganPart #MS1
    Clinical relevance→Site sampled during cervical screening; transformation zone is clinically important in cervical neoplasia. #Clinical #OrganPart #MS1
    
13
    Identification→External os of the uterus (ostium) #Identification #Opening #MS1
    Organ→Opening of the cervical canal into the vagina. #Organ #Opening #MS1
    Location→At the inferior end of the cervix within the vaginal part. #Location #Opening #MS1
    Function→Allows passage between the cervical canal and vagina, including menstrual flow and sperm transit. #Function #Opening #MS1
    
14
    Identification→Uterine tube (Fallopian tube) #Identification #Tube #MS1
    Function→Transports the oocyte/early embryo; usual site of fertilisation is the ampulla. #Function #Tube #MS1
    Parts→Infundibulum with fimbriae, ampulla, isthmus and intramural/uterine part. #Parts #Tube #MS1
    Course→Extends from the uterine horn laterally within the superior free margin of the broad ligament toward the ovary. #Course #Tube #MS1
    Blood supply→Tubal branches of uterine and ovarian arteries. #BloodSupply #Tube #MS1
    Clinical relevance→Common site of ectopic pregnancy, especially the ampulla. #Clinical #Tube #MS1
    
15
    Identification→Ovary #Identification #Organ #MS1
    Function→Produces oocytes and secretes ovarian hormones including oestrogens and progesterone. #Function #Organ #MS1
    Location→Usually lies on the lateral pelvic wall in the ovarian fossa. #Location #Organ #MS1
    Attachments→Attached to uterus by ovarian ligament and to lateral pelvic wall by suspensory ligament of ovary; mesovarium attaches it to broad ligament. #Attachments #Organ #MS1
    Blood supply→Ovarian artery from abdominal aorta, with anastomosis to ovarian branch of uterine artery. #BloodSupply #Organ #MS1
    Lymphatic drainage→Para-aortic (lumbar) lymph nodes. #Lymphatics #Organ #MS1
    
16
    Identification→Ovarian ligament #Identification #Ligament #MS1
    Attachments→Extends from medial pole of ovary to lateral angle of the uterus, just inferior to the uterine tube. #Attachments #Ligament #MS1
    Function→Helps anchor the ovary to the uterus. #Function #Ligament #MS1
    Embryology→Derived from the gubernaculum. #Embryology #Ligament #MS1
    
17
    Identification→Round ligament of the uterus #Identification #Ligament #MS1
    Attachments→Extends from the uterine horn, passes through the deep inguinal ring and inguinal canal, and ends in connective tissue of the labium majus. #Attachments #Ligament #MS1
    Function→Helps maintain uterine anteversion. #Function #Ligament #MS1
    Embryology→Derived from the gubernaculum. #Embryology #Ligament #MS1
    Relations→Runs within the broad ligament before entering the inguinal canal. #Relations #Ligament #MS1
    
18
    Identification→Rectum #Identification #Organ #MS1
    Function→Stores faeces before defecation. #Function #Organ #MS1
    Course→Begins at the rectosigmoid junction around S3 and follows the sacral curve to the anorectal junction. #Course #Organ #MS1
    Relations→Lies posterior to the uterus and vagina; the rectouterine pouch lies between rectum and uterus. #Relations #Organ #MS1
    Blood supply→Superior rectal artery with contributions from middle and inferior rectal arteries. #BloodSupply #Organ #MS1
    Lymphatic drainage→Upper rectum mainly to pararectal and inferior mesenteric nodes; lower rectum also to internal iliac nodes. #Lymphatics #Organ #MS1
    
19
    Identification→External anal sphincter muscle #Identification #Muscle #MS1
    Actions→Provides voluntary constriction of the anal canal and contributes to faecal continence. #Action #Muscle #MS1
    Innervation→Inferior rectal nerves from pudendal nerve (S2–S4), with contributions from S4. #Innervation #Muscle #MS1
    Origin→Encircles the anal canal and attaches anteriorly to the perineal body. #Origin #Muscle #MS1
    Insertion→Posteriorly attaches to the anococcygeal ligament/body and coccyx region. #Insertion #Muscle #MS1
    
20
    Identification→Common iliac artery #Identification #Artery #MS1
    Origin→Bifurcation of the abdominal aorta, usually at L4. #Origin #Artery #MS1
    Course→Runs inferolaterally toward the sacroiliac joint. #Course #Artery #MS1
    Branches→Terminates as external and internal iliac arteries. #Branches #Artery #MS1
    Supply→Pelvis and lower limb through its terminal branches. #Supply #Artery #MS1
    Relations→Common iliac veins lie mainly posterior; ureter crosses near the iliac bifurcation. #Relations #Artery #MS1
    
21
    Identification→Common iliac vein #Identification #Vein #MS1
    Formation→Union of the external and internal iliac veins. #Formation #Vein #MS1
    Course→Ascends toward the right side of L5 where the right and left common iliac veins unite. #Course #Vein #MS1
    Termination→Right and left common iliac veins form the inferior vena cava. #Termination #Vein #MS1
    Drainage→Pelvis and lower limb. #Drainage #Vein #MS1
    Relations→Common iliac arteries lie anterior; the right common iliac artery crosses anterior to the left common iliac vein. #Relations #Vein #MS1
    
22
    Identification→External iliac artery #Identification #Artery #MS1
    Origin→Terminal branch of the common iliac artery. #Origin #Artery #MS1
    Course→Runs along the medial border of psoas major toward the inguinal ligament, where it continues as the femoral artery. #Course #Artery #MS1
    Branches→Inferior epigastric and deep circumflex iliac arteries are its principal branches. #Branches #Artery #MS1
    Supply→Main arterial route to the lower limb; also supplies lower anterior abdominal wall through branches. #Supply #Artery #MS1
    Relations→External iliac vein lies mainly medial; femoral nerve lies lateral and outside the vascular sheath. #Relations #Artery #MS1
    
23
    Identification→External iliac vein #Identification #Vein #MS1
    Origin→Continuation of the femoral vein proximal to the inguinal ligament. #Origin #Vein #MS1
    Course→Ascends along the pelvic brim, generally medial to the external iliac artery. #Course #Vein #MS1
    Tributaries→Receives inferior epigastric and deep circumflex iliac veins. #Tributaries #Vein #MS1
    Termination→Joins the internal iliac vein to form the common iliac vein near the sacroiliac joint. #Termination #Vein #MS1
    Drainage→Drains most of the lower limb and part of the lower anterior abdominal wall. #Drainage #Vein #MS1
    
24
    Identification→Internal iliac artery #Identification #Artery #MS1
    Origin→Terminal branch of the common iliac artery near the sacroiliac joint. #Origin #Artery #MS1
    Course→Descends into the lesser pelvis and usually divides into anterior and posterior divisions. #Course #Artery #MS1
    Branches→Posterior division gives iliolumbar, lateral sacral and superior gluteal arteries; anterior division gives pelvic visceral and perineal branches including uterine, vaginal, obturator, internal pudendal and inferior gluteal arteries. #Branches #Artery #MS1
    Supply→Pelvic walls and viscera, gluteal region, perineum and parts of the medial thigh. #Supply #Artery #MS1
    Relations→Lies anterior to the internal iliac vein; branching pattern is variable. #Relations #Artery #MS1
    
25
    Identification→Internal iliac vein #Identification #Vein #MS1
    Formation→Formed by tributaries corresponding broadly to branches of the internal iliac artery and by pelvic venous plexuses. #Formation #Vein #MS1
    Course→Ascends from the pelvis posterior/medial to the internal iliac artery. #Course #Vein #MS1
    Termination→Joins the external iliac vein to form the common iliac vein. #Termination #Vein #MS1
    Drainage→Drains pelvic viscera, pelvic walls, gluteal region and perineum. #Drainage #Vein #MS1
    Relations→Connected to extensive valveless pelvic venous plexuses around pelvic organs. #Relations #Vein #MS1
    
26
    Identification→Peritoneum #Identification #SerousMembrane #MS1
    Definition→Serous membrane lining the abdominopelvic cavity and covering intraperitoneal viscera. #Definition #SerousMembrane #MS1
    Layers→Parietal peritoneum lines the body wall; visceral peritoneum covers organs. #Layers #SerousMembrane #MS1
    Relations→In the female pelvis, reflects between bladder and uterus to form the vesicouterine pouch and between uterus and rectum to form the rectouterine pouch (pouch of Douglas). #Relations #SerousMembrane #MS1
    Innervation→Parietal peritoneum has somatic innervation and is pain-sensitive; visceral peritoneum carries visceral afferent fibres with autonomic nerves. #Innervation #SerousMembrane #MS1
